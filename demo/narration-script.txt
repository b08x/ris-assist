CHAPTER 1 — The problem
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

An order interface drops at three in the morning. The ER is screaming about a stat head CT. Someone has to tell the radiologists where to read from, the techs which paper downtime process to start, and the oncoming shift what they're walking into.

Nobody has a template for any of this.

So it isn't one message. It's a grid. Event class down one side, audience across the top. And most sites have never written most of these down.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CHAPTER 2 — Reading the grid
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Here is the coverage grid. The filled cells are the seven templates someone actually documented five years ago. The hatched cells are the void.

Look at the next-shift column. Empty. The incoming analyst gets whatever the outgoing one managed to type into a handoff note before passing out.

That isn't a broken process. That's just Tuesday.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CHAPTER 3 — The happy path
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Start with one they do have. Unplanned outage, radiologists. Here are the facts we know: the order interface went down at six fifteen in the morning, we have an incident number, and we do not have a cause or an estimated time to repair.

Radiologists don't care that your broker is dropping ACKs. They care that they can't dictate. The draft gives them the PACS worklist workaround and the downtime phone extension. Three sentences. Because by sentence four, they're already calling the helpdesk to yell at you.

Notice the ETA is blank. It doesn't invent one, because guessing an ETA is how you lose your job.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CHAPTER 4 — The gap
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Now the interesting one. Same outage, next shift, and there is no template for this cell.

Here's a gap. No template. An AI that wants to please you will hallucinate a distribution list and invent a downtime procedure. This one doesn't.

It flags what it borrowed and tells you it's provisional. A confident hallucination at 4 AM doesn't just look bad. It creates a patient safety event.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CHAPTER 5 — Your own facts
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Add a suspected cause, it marks it suspected. Confirm it, it marks it confirmed. It tracks your certainty instead of flattening it.

The form numbers, the paper colors, the extensions — those come from your site profile, not a training corpus.

You don't want a generic apology. You want the exact sequence of numbers that makes the paging system stop beeping.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
CHAPTER 6 — The close
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

So the deliverable isn't the drafts. It's the grid. It shows a support lead exactly which of their notifications are standardized, and which are still living in somebody's head. And it turns that into a list of things to fix.

The site in this demo is invented. The templates, the form numbers, the phone extension, the incident number — all fictional.

Nothing here came from a real hospital, and no patient data touches any part of this.
