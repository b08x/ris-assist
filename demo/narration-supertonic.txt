@section 01_problem

Every hospital sends notifications when a system goes down. The order interface fails, and someone has to tell the radiologists, and the technologists, and the shift coming on next. Each of those groups needs to hear something different.

The radiologists need to know where to read from. The technologists need to know which paper process to start. Leadership needs to know how big this is.

@pause 0.4

So it isn't one message. It's a grid. Event class down one side, audience across the top. And most sites have never written most of these down.

@pause 0.8

@section 02_grid

This is one site's actual coverage. The filled cells are notifications they have a template for. Seven of them. The hatched cells are the ones they don't.

@pause 0.3

Look at the next shift column. Nothing. Every unplanned outage, every planned maintenance window, and the incoming analyst gets whatever the outgoing one remembered to type in the handoff notes.

@pause 0.5

That's not a criticism of the site. That's what normal looks like.

@pause 0.8

@section 03_happy_path

Start with one they do have. Unplanned outage, radiologists. Here are the facts we know: the order interface went down at 6:15 AM, we have an incident number, and we do not have a cause or an estimated time to repair.

The draft leads with what the radiologist has to do, not with what broke. It tells them to read direct from the P A C S worklist. It gives them the phone dictation line, extension 4412. Three sentences, because this site's profile says radiologists read the first two lines and stop.

@pause 0.4

And notice what is missing. There is no estimated time to repair, because nobody gave it one. It's listed underneath as an unfilled field instead of being invented.

@pause 0.6

That distinction is the whole product.

@pause 0.9

@section 04_gap

Now the interesting one. Same outage, next shift, and there is no template for this cell.

@pause 0.5

A tool that wanted to look impressive would just write something. This one tells you first. It says which template it borrowed from, it says the structure is provisional, and then it gives you the draft anyway, so you are not stuck.

@pause 0.6

That's the behavior worth arguing about in a support organization. Not whether the writing is good. Whether you can tell, at a glance, which parts of the output the tool actually knew, and which parts it borrowed.

@pause 0.7

A confident wrong answer costs you more than no answer at all. And it costs you later, when someone has already acted on it.

@pause 1.2

@section 05_your_facts

Change the facts and it changes with them. Add a suspected cause, and the draft marks it suspected, because that's how you phrased it. Say it's confirmed, and it says confirmed. It tracks your certainty rather than flattening it.

@pause 0.4

And none of the site language here comes from the model. The downtime packet form number, the yellow requisition slips, the dictation extension — all of that comes out of a configuration file for this site.

Swap the file, and the voice changes with it. The tool is generic. The output is not.

@pause 0.8

@section 06_close

So the deliverable isn't the drafts. It's the grid. It shows a support lead exactly which of their notifications are standardized, and which are still living in somebody's head. And it turns that into a list of things to fix.

@pause 0.6

The site in this demo is invented. The templates, the form numbers, the phone extension, the incident number — all fictional.

@pause 0.4

Nothing here came from a real hospital, and no patient data touches any part of this.
